The deciding constraint here is form factor, not compute: a Sense HAT is a Raspberry Pi HAT — it requires the standard 40-pin GPIO header and physically stacks on a Raspberry Pi board. It cannot attach to or work with a Jetson or Coral device.

1. raspberrypi4-64 — has the standard 40-pin GPIO header the Sense HAT plugs into, and CHI@Edge's Pi 4 image has well-documented I2C/Sense HAT support for reading its HTS221 temperature/humidity sensor.
2. raspberrypi5 — same 40-pin header and I2C bus; a fine alternative if the Pi 4 isn't free, though confirm the sense-hat library/kernel overlay is present on the Pi 5 image since I'm less certain of parity there than on the Pi 4.

I'm deliberately not giving a third option: no other CHI@Edge device type (jetson-nano, jetson-xavier-nx-devkit-emmc, jetson-orin-nano-devkit-nvme, jetson-agx-orin-devkit-64gb, coral-dev) has the GPIO header layout needed to seat a Sense HAT. If both Pi boards are unavailable, this task can't be done on CHI@Edge right now — that's a genuine dead end, not something a Jetson or Coral device can substitute for.

```python
# Example: read temperature and humidity from a Sense HAT
from sense_hat import SenseHat

sense = SenseHat()
temperature = sense.get_temperature()
humidity = sense.get_humidity()
print(f"Temperature: {temperature:.1f} C, Humidity: {humidity:.1f} %")
```
